# Lipoprotein(a)
## Summary
Lipoprotein(a) is a variant of low-density lipoprotein that contains a protein called apolipoprotein(a), which is a risk factor for atherosclerosis and related diseases. It was discovered in 1963 and the human gene encoding apolipoprotein(a) was cloned in 1987. Lipoprotein(a) has been identified as a contributor to the process of atherogenesis, and its levels have been found to be highly heritable. High levels of lipoprotein(a) have been correlated with an increased risk of coronary heart disease, cardiovascular disease, and stroke.
## Key Concepts
* Lipoprotein(a) is a low-density lipoprotein variant containing apolipoprotein(a)
* It is a risk factor for atherosclerosis and related diseases
* High levels of lipoprotein(a) are correlated with an increased risk of coronary heart disease, cardiovascular disease, and stroke
* Lipoprotein(a) is highly heritable and mainly controlled by the LPA gene
* Apolipoprotein(a) isoforms vary in size due to a size polymorphism
## Details
Lipoprotein(a) consists of an LDL-like particle and apolipoprotein(a), which is bound covalently to the apoB contained in the outer shell of the particle. The size of apolipoprotein(a) isoforms varies due to a size polymorphism, and this variation is expressed on the protein level as well. The level of lipoprotein(a) in the blood can vary greatly between individuals, and it has been found that populations of African descent have higher mean lipoprotein(a) plasma concentrations compared to other populations. Lipoprotein(a) contributes to the process of atherogenesis by competing with plasminogen for its binding site, leading to reduced fibrinolysis, and by stimulating secretion of PAI-1, leading to thrombogenesis. It also carries atherosclerosis-causing cholesterol and binds atherogenic pro-inflammatory oxidized phospholipids.

## Part 1

# Cognitive Changes during Prolonged Stay at High Altitude
## Summary
This study investigates the correlation of peripheral blood hypersensitive C-reactive protein (hsCRP) levels with cognitive decline due to high-altitude exposure. The research was conducted on 100 male military participants who had never been to high altitude. The results suggest that cognitive deterioration occurs during the acute period of exposure to high altitude and may recover after extended stay at high altitude. Plasma hsCRP levels were found to be inversely correlated with cognitive performance, indicating that hsCRP may be a potential biomarker for predicting high-altitude-induced cognitive dysfunction.
## Key Concepts
* High altitude exposure leads to cognitive decline
* Hypersensitive C-reactive protein (hsCRP) levels are inversely correlated with cognitive performance
* P300 and N200 latencies are prolonged at high altitude
* Brain oxygen saturation increases at high altitude
* Cognitive function recovers after extended stay at high altitude
## Details
The study consisted of a longitudinal design, where participants were assessed at multiple time points during their stay at high altitude. The assessments included cognitive tests, such as the digit symbol substitution test (DSST), and biochemical tests, such as hsCRP and interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels. The results showed that high altitude exposure led to increased brain oxygen saturation, prolonged P300 and N200 latencies, and injured cognitive functions. However, these effects were found to recover in varying degrees after 1 and 3 months of stay at high altitude. The study also found a strong inverse correlation between hsCRP levels and cognitive performance, suggesting that hsCRP may be a potential biomarker for predicting high-altitude-induced cognitive dysfunction.
## Sources
* Hu, S. L., Xiong, W., Dai, Z. Q., Zhao, H. L., & Feng, H. (2016). Cognitive Changes during Prolonged Stay at High Altitude and Its Correlation with C-Reactive Protein. PLOS ONE, 11(1), e0146290. doi: 10.1371/journal.pone.0146290

## Part 2

# Cognitive Changes at High Altitude and C-Reactive Protein
## Summary
This study examines the relationship between high altitude exposure and cognitive changes, focusing on the role of C-reactive protein (CRP) as a potential biomarker. The research reveals that high altitude increases plasma CRP levels, which are associated with cognitive impairment. However, after prolonged exposure to high altitude, cognitive function recovers, possibly due to acclimatization. The study suggests that CRP may be a marker of cognitive decline resulting from high altitude exposure.
## Key Concepts
* High altitude exposure affects cognitive function
* C-reactive protein (CRP) is a potential biomarker for cognitive decline
* CRP levels increase with high altitude exposure and decrease after prolonged exposure
* Cognitive function recovers after acclimatization to high altitude
* Inflammation and cognitive impairment are linked
## Details
The study used near-infrared spectroscopy to measure cerebral oxygenation and event-related potentials (ERPs) to assess cognitive function. The results showed that high altitude exposure increased CRP levels, which were associated with cognitive impairment. The study also found that CRP levels decreased after prolonged exposure to high altitude, and cognitive function recovered. The researchers suggest that CRP may be a useful marker for monitoring cognitive changes at high altitude. The study's findings have implications for occupational health and public health, particularly for individuals who work or travel to high-altitude areas.

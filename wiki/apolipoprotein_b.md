# Apolipoprotein B
## Summary
Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) is a protein encoded by the APOB gene in humans, commonly used to detect the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. It occurs in two main isoforms, ApoB48 and ApoB100, which are synthesized by the small intestine and liver, respectively. ApoB plays a crucial role in carrying fat molecules, including cholesterol, around the body to all cells within all tissues. The measurement of ApoB concentrations is a superior indicator of vascular/heart disease driving physiology compared to total cholesterol or LDL-cholesterol. ApoB has been linked to various genetic disorders, including heart disease, hypobetalipoproteinemia, and familial hypercholesterolemia.

## Details
Apolipoprotein B is the primary apolipoprotein of chylomicrons, VLDL, Lp(a), IDL, and LDL particles, which are responsible for carrying fat molecules around the body. The ApoB protein is composed of 4563 amino acids, including a 27-amino acid signal peptide and a 4536-amino acid mature protein. The protein is encoded by a single mRNA transcript larger than 16 kb, which is subject to RNA editing. The RNA editing process involves the creation of a stop codon (UAA) at residue 2153, resulting in the translation of a truncated protein, ApoB48. ApoB48 lacks the LDL receptor binding region, which is present in ApoB100. The function of ApoB48 is specific to the small intestine, where it plays a key role in fat absorption and the synthesis, assembly, and secretion of chylomicrons.

# Apolipoprotein B
## Summary
Apolipoprotein B (ApoB) is a protein encoded by the APOB gene in humans. It is a primary apolipoprotein of chylomicrons, VLDL, Lp(a), IDL, and LDL particles, responsible for carrying fat molecules around the body. ApoB is used to detect the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The protein occurs in two main isoforms, ApoB48 and ApoB100, which are coded by the APOB gene and a single mRNA transcript. ApoB48 is generated by RNA editing, which creates a stop codon and results in a truncated protein.

## Details
Apolipoprotein B is the primary apolipoprotein of chylomicrons, VLDL, Lp(a), IDL, and LDL particles. It is responsible for carrying fat molecules, including cholesterol, around the body to all cells within all tissues. ApoB100 is the largest of the apoB group of proteins, consisting of 4563 amino acids. ApoB48 is a unique protein in chylomicrons from the small intestine, and it is involved in the synthesis, assembly, and secretion of chylomicrons. The function of ApoB100 is as a ligand for activation of the LDL-R, while ApoB48 lacks this LDL-R binding region and has a function specific to the small intestine. RNA editing of ApoB mRNA results in a codon change, creating an in-frame stop codon and leading to translation of a truncated protein, ApoB48.

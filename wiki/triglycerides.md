# Triglycerides
## Summary
Triglycerides are esters derived from glycerol and three fatty acids, and are the main constituents of body fat in humans and other vertebrates, as well as vegetable fat. They are also present in the blood and are a major component of human skin oils. Triglycerides can be classified into saturated and unsaturated types, with unsaturated fats having one or more C=C groups. The classification of triglycerides is important for human nutrition, with saturated fats having a higher melting point than unsaturated ones.

## Key Concepts
* Triglycerides are composed of glycerol and three fatty acids
* Saturated and unsaturated triglycerides have different properties and uses
* Triglycerides are the main components of body fat and vegetable fat
* Triglycerides can be classified into different types based on their fatty acid composition
* Triglycerides have various commercial applications, including in food, paint, and biodiesel production

## Details
Triglycerides are formed through the condensation reaction of glycerol with three fatty acids. The fatty acids can be the same or different, and the positions of the fatty acids are specified using stereospecific numbering. The chain lengths of the fatty acid groups vary in naturally occurring triglycerides, with long-chain triglycerides containing 16, 18, or 20 carbon atoms. Triglycerides can also be classified into saturated and unsaturated types, with saturated fats having no C=C groups and unsaturated fats having one or more C=C groups. Unsaturated fats tend to have a lower melting point than saturated analogues and are often liquid at room temperature.
